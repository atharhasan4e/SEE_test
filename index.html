Expert Elicitation Survey

Structured expert elicitation to generate resource utilisation estimates for the patient journey in idiopathic short stature (ISS)

Welcome

This exercise uses the 'Chips and Bins' method to gather expert judgments.


You will answer 19 questions sequentially.

For each question, distribute 20 chips across the bins to represent your judgment and uncertainty.


Training - Chips and Bins Method


What is Structured Expert Elicitation?

Structured Expert Elicitation (SEE) is a method for obtaining judgments from experts that minimizes bias and reflects uncertainty.


How to Use Chips and Bins

You will be presented with questions and a grid format:

  • X-axis: Range of possible values (0-100%)
  • Y-axis: Probability or certainty
  • Chips: You have 20 chips to distribute
  • Each chip: Represents 5% likelihood

Instructions:

  1. Click on the plot to place chips in bins
  2. Each bin represents a 10% range (e.g., 0-10%, 10-20%, etc.)
  3. Distribute all 20 chips to represent your judgment
  4. The more chips in a bin, the more likely you think that range is
  5. Click 'Submit Answer' when you've placed all chips

Example Interpretations:

Scenario 1: High Certainty

If you're certain the answer is between 40-50%, place all 20 chips in that bin.


Scenario 2: Moderate Uncertainty

If you think it's likely between 30-60% but most likely 40-50%, place more chips in the 40-50% bin and fewer in adjacent bins.


Scenario 3: High Uncertainty

If you're very uncertain, spread chips more evenly across multiple bins.


Important Points:

  • There are no right or wrong answers
  • Express your uncertainty through chip distribution
  • You can revise your answers before final submission
  • All 20 chips must be used before submitting
